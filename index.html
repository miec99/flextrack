The problem

Injuries rarely come from nowhere

Most muscle injuries are non-contact. They build up out of things a team can see coming: a player returning from an old strain, a week where training suddenly doubles, a run of short nights, a calf that has been tight for days.

Elite clubs pay sports scientists to watch for these patterns. School, academy and club athletes usually have no one doing it. The warning signs are there, but they are scattered across a coach's memory, a physio's notes and an athlete's own body.

FlexTrack puts those signals in one place and maps them onto the body, so the question changes from "is this athlete tired?" to "which part of this athlete needs attention today?"

    How the score works

    Phase 4 · Concept

    The FlexTrack patch

    The check-in relies on what athletes report. The patch adds what their muscles report. It sticks over the region the map flags (a hamstring, a calf) and measures local fatigue during the session.

    Surface EMG: muscle fatigue

    Proven in wearables

    As a muscle tires, the frequency of its electrical signal shifts lower. Research groups have already built EMG patches that track this in real time during exercise. [8]

    Motion sensor (IMU): load and asymmetry

    Proven in wearables

    Counts jumps, sprints and landings, and spots when one leg starts doing more work than the other.

    Sweat rate and sodium

    On the market

    Single-use sweat patches already measure these for hydration. [9] FlexTrack would use them as context, not as an injury signal.

    Skin temperature

    Context only

    Helps interpret the other signals in heat, which matters for athletes training in the Gulf.

    Sweat lactate

    Research stage

    Left out for now. Sweat lactate does not yet track blood lactate reliably, and reviews call for more validation. [10]

    From pitch to prototype

    What changed when I checked the science

    The first version of FlexTrack was a competition pitch. Turning it into something real meant testing every claim against the research and changing what did not hold up.

    In the 2025 pitchNowWhy
    "Nano-sensors" measure micro-strain, hydration, lactate and blood flowSurface EMG, motion, temperature and sweat sensorsThese are proven in wearables today. Sweat lactate is still unreliable. [8][10]
    "AI-powered" from day oneTransparent score now; machine learning after the pilotA model is only as good as its data, and small datasets are the main weakness in published injury models. [7]
    Patch firstCheck-in first, patch laterThe strongest predictor (past injury) needs no hardware. Athletes can use the check-in this season.
    Headline injury figures without sourcesEvery number on this page is citedInjury cost and incidence figures now come from UEFA's injury study and Howden's injury index. [1][2]
    Built around one elite clubBuilt for school, academy and club athletesElite clubs already have sports scientists. Younger athletes usually have no one watching these signals.
